BERT & MASKED AUTOENCODERS (MAE)

Mask up to 75% of input tokens or image patches, process unmasked patches efficiently with Transformer encoders, and reconstruct missing pixels via lightweight decoders.

01 — Theory & Foundations

House of Knowledge

Masking ratios, asymmetric encoder-decoder architectures, and pixel-level MSE reconstruction losses.

🎭

1. High Ratio Patch Masking (\(\rho\))

Randomly drops a high ratio (\(\rho = 75\%\) for MAE, \(\rho = 15\%\) for BERT) of input patches \(\mathbf{x}_m\) leaving unmasked set \(\mathbf{x}_vis\):

$$\mathbf{x}_{vis} \subset \mathbf{x}, \quad |\mathbf{x}_{vis}| = (1 - \rho) |\mathbf{x}|$$
⚡

2. Asymmetric Transformer Encoder

Heavy ViT encoder processes only visible unmasked patches \(\mathbf{x}_{vis}\), reducing compute costs by \(4\times\):

$$\mathbf{z}_{vis} = \text{Encoder}(\mathbf{x}_{vis} + \mathbf{e}_{vis})$$
🧩

3. Mask Token Insertion & Decoder

Shared learnable mask tokens \(\mathbf{m}\) are padded into latent representations before lightweight ViT decoder reconstruction:

$$\hat{\mathbf{x}} = \text{Decoder}([\mathbf{z}_{vis}; \mathbf{m}] + \mathbf{e}_{full})$$
🎯

4. Normalized Pixel MSE Loss

Reconstruction loss is evaluated exclusively over masked patch pixels \(\mathbf{x}_m\) using normalized pixel target values:

$$\mathcal{L}_{MAE} = \frac{1}{|M|} \sum_{i \in M} \|\hat{x}_i - x_{norm, i}\|_2^2$$

💡 Interview & Production Guidelines

Q: Why does MAE require 75% masking vs BERT's 15%?

Images possess high spatial redundancy. Low masking ratios (e.g. 15%) allow easy linear interpolation from adjacent pixels. 75% masking forces the model to learn holistic high-level semantic structures.

Q: Why normalize target patch pixels per patch?

Normalizing patch mean and variance (\(\mu=0, \sigma=1\)) enhances local contrast representation learning and prevents global illumination bias from dominating loss.
